# Reading your first month of data

If you followed the [starter guide](/guide/starter-guide), you already did the hard part: **tracking almost every day for a month.** This is the lesson that guide promised. Five minutes to turn those entries into a clear picture — one that helps instead of discouraging you.

The goal today is not to change anything yet. It is to understand what your numbers say.

---

## Read the totals without judging yourself

Open **Reports**, pick **This month**, and look at **Expense by Category**. You will see your categories ordered from largest to smallest, all converted to your household's base currency.

Almost everyone's first reaction is the same: "I didn't know I spent that much on that." If that happens to you, good — it means tracking worked. That number is not a grade. It is information you didn't have before.

Three rules for this first read:

- **Look, don't fix.** Today is not about spending less; it is about seeing where the money goes.
- **A partial month still counts.** If you started on the 17th, you read two weeks. That is already more truth than you had.
- **Rough categories are fine.** If something landed in "Other," it doesn't matter. The big bars don't change because of that.

Any first month looks something like this: Home C$11,800, Food C$9,500, Transport C$2,400, Personal and fun C$3,100, Health and other C$1,600. None of that is good or bad. It is the map of a real month. Yours will look different — and that's fine.

---

## Fixed vs. variable: why the difference matters

The distinction that helps you most this month:

- **Fixed:** repeats almost identically and you decide on it a few times a year. Rent (say C$9,500), internet (C$1,100), the phone plan. You know the amount before it arrives.
- **Variable:** changes every week. Eating out, corner-store runs, buses and taxis, top-ups, treats. Nobody knows the total until the month closes.

They change in different ways. A fixed cost gets reviewed once — you negotiate or switch plans — and the saving repeats on its own. A variable cost moves through dozens of small daily decisions.

And there is a practical consequence: **a small change in your biggest variable category moves more money than perfect control of a small one.** If Food is C$9,500 and Personal is C$1,200, cooking twice more a week does more than cutting a tiny treat entirely. That's not a judgment on the treat — it's arithmetic.

In **Reports**, notice which of your two or three biggest bars are fixed and which are variable. That tells you where you have room to move week by week.

## The small costs that repeat

You already know your big costs by heart. The ones you don't see are the ones that look tiny alone and show up when added together. Run the math on your own **Transactions** numbers:

- The C$50 coffee on the way to work, 22 days: **C$1,100 a month**
- The C$40 soda, four times a week: **about C$640**
- The C$120 taxi when it rains, six times a month: **C$720**

None of them hurts alone. Together they are about C$2,500 — more than all the bus fares in the month.

The point is not to cut them. If that morning coffee is what keeps you going, it stays. The point is **to see the monthly total before deciding**. Spending you choose with the number in front of you is a decision; the same spending without seeing it is just habit. That difference is exactly what your month of tracking just gave you.

## Compare money in with money out

Look at the **Monthly Balance** in **Reports**: what came in minus what went out. The result falls into one of three cases, and all three are normal in a first month:

- **Surplus.** C$32,000 in, C$29,400 out: C$2,600 left. You have your starting point.
- **Break-even.** C$28,000 in, C$27,800 out. The month held with no margin: big costs need planning.
- **Shortfall.** C$26,000 in, C$30,400 out: C$4,400 missing. You used to suspect something didn't add up; now you know it's C$4,400 and which categories hold it. That's the job of the first month.

Two notes for reading this number right. Transfers between your own accounts (moving money to cash) are not expenses and don't reduce the balance. And if your household uses more than one currency, **Reports** already converts everything to the base currency.

## What a normal first month looks like

If any of this describes you, your month is normal:

- **It's incomplete.** You started mid-month or forgot days. Normal.
- **Home + Food take 60–75%.** That's typical, not a sign of excess.
- **There's one surprise.** Almost always eating out, corner stores, or transport. The surprise is the product, not the problem.
- **One big one-off skews everything.** The car repair, a birthday, a doctor visit. Your "typical" month shows up after two or three months of data.
- **As a couple, it doesn't add up at first.** One of you tracked more, something stayed in a private account. The ten-minute monthly review from the [starter guide](/guide/starter-guide) exists exactly for this.

There is no perfect first month to compare yourself against. There are real months, and yours already gave you what you needed.

Don't create a dozen budgets at once. Pick **one or two categories** that surprised you and watch them for one more week with the number in mind. That's all for now.
